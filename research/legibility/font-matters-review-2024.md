# Font Matters: Font Types, Attention, and Reading

**Citation:** Gadhvi, M. A., Baranwal, A., Chalakapure, A., & Dixit, A. (2024). Font Matters: Deciphering the Impact of Font Types on Attention and Reading. Cureus.

**Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11156575/

## Summary

In a cross-sectional study of 30 healthy adults comparing serif, sans-serif, and script fonts, serif fonts (e.g., Times New Roman) produced significantly faster completion and fewer omissions on a letter-cancellation (visual attention) task (p < 0.05), while font type had no significant effect on working-memory accuracy on an N-back test (91.9-93.7% across font types, p > 0.05). For Seshat, this suggests typeface choice may meaningfully affect sustained visual-attention tasks like scanning/proofreading a card but is unlikely to materially affect memory encoding/recall during spaced-repetition study itself — supporting the app's stance that typeface is a legibility/comfort preference rather than a lever expected to change learning outcomes.
