# Utilising Psychophysical Techniques to Investigate the Effects of Age, Type Design, Size and Display Polarities on Glance Legibility

**Citation:** Dobres, J., Chahine, N., Reimer, B., Gould, D., Mehler, B., & Coughlin, J. F. (2016). Utilising psychophysical techniques to investigate the effects of age, type design, size and display polarities on glance legibility. *Ergonomics*, 59(10), 1377–1391.

**Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5213401/

## Summary

Fetched successfully (full text, via PMC). Using an adaptive-staircase lexical decision task comparing a humanist typeface (Frutiger) against a square grotesque typeface (Eurostile), the study found the humanist design required about 8.8% shorter presentation durations to be recognized (i.e., was more legible at a glance), black-on-white text was substantially more legible than white-on-black (~38.6% lower thresholds), and legibility degraded more for the square grotesque font than the humanist font as size shrank (62.1% vs. 26.4% threshold increase from 4mm to 3mm). Older participants needed longer presentation durations overall, and were disproportionately hurt by the less-legible typeface/size/polarity combinations. For Seshat, this supports prioritizing humanist-style, open-letterform typefaces (its roster includes humanist sans like Atkinson Hyperlegible and Inter) and dark-text-on-light-background as the default polarity, and suggests legibility margins matter most at small sizes and for older users — relevant to Seshat's 11.5–13pt body size range.
