# Serif vs. Sans Serif Typeface and Web Usability

**Citation:** Vecino, S., Mehtali, J., de Andrés, J., Gonzalez-Rodriguez, M., & Fernandez-Lanvin, D. (2022). How Does Serif vs. Sans Serif Typeface Impact the Usability of E-Commerce Websites? PeerJ Computer Science, 8:e1139.

**Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9680897/

## Summary

In a study of 246 participants comparing serif and sans-serif versions of the same font family on a prototype e-commerce site, the researchers found no statistically significant differences in reading comprehension, task completion time, or reading speed between serif and sans-serif typefaces (p > 0.05); female participants showed a significant preference for the serif variant (p = 0.026). For Seshat, this supports offering both serif (Source Serif 4, Georgia) and sans-serif (Atkinson Hyperlegible, Verdana, Inter) options as a matter of user preference rather than measurable reading-performance advantage — the choice can reasonably be left to the individual, since the paper found no evidence that sans-serif is inherently more usable on screen despite its prevalence on major web platforms.
