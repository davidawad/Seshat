# Different Fonts Increase Reading Speed for Different Individuals

**Citation:** Wallace, S., Bylinskii, Z., Dobres, J., Kerr, B., Berlow, S., Treitman, R., Kumawat, N., Arpin, K., Miller, D. B., Huang, J., & Sawyer, B. D. (2022). Towards Individuated Reading Experiences: Different Fonts Increase Reading Speed for Different Individuals. *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction (TOCHI)*, 29(4), Article 38, 1–56.

**Link:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3502222

## Summary

Fetched successfully (full text, via browser). In a large crowdsourced study (hundreds of participants, 16 fonts), the authors found reading speed (WPM) increased by 35% between each participant's fastest and slowest font, with no corresponding loss in comprehension — and that different individuals had different optimal fonts, i.e. "one font does not fit all." Notably, a participant's most-preferred font was rarely their fastest-reading font, and the effect was largest for the fastest readers, who also showed the biggest speed swings across fonts (nearly every participant showed at least a small effect size, per Cohen's d, when comparing their fastest vs. slowest font). For Seshat, this directly supports offering multiple typeface choices (Atkinson Hyperlegible, Verdana, Inter, Source Serif 4, Georgia) rather than a single "best" font, since individual reading-speed optima vary and self-reported preference is an unreliable proxy for actual effectiveness.
