# Assessing the Impact of Typeface Design in a Text-Rich Automotive User Interface

**Citation:** Reimer, B., Mehler, B., Dobres, J., Coughlin, J. F., Matteson, S., Gould, D., Chahine, N., & Levantovsky, V. (2014). Assessing the impact of typeface design in a text-rich automotive user interface. *Ergonomics*, 57(11), 1643–1658.

**Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4267594/

## Summary

Fetched successfully (full text, via PMC). In a driving-simulator study comparing a humanist typeface (Frutiger) to a square grotesque typeface (Eurostile) for in-vehicle menu text, male drivers using the humanist typeface showed a 10.6% reduction in total glance time, 15.7% faster response times, and 14% fewer glances needed to complete tasks; both genders combined showed about 3.1% lower error rates with the humanist typeface, though female drivers showed little to no difference in the high-contrast condition. The authors attribute the gains to open letterforms, better character spacing, and more distinctive letter shapes reducing visual/cognitive demand. For Seshat, though the automotive/glance-reading context differs from sustained flashcard reading, the underlying mechanism — humanist typeface design with open letterforms and spacing reduces visual processing demand and errors — reinforces the choice to include humanist sans-serif options (Atkinson Hyperlegible, Inter) among Seshat's typeface presets for users who prioritize fast, low-error recognition.
