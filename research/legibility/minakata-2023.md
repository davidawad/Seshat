# Serifs, Stroke Contrast, and Low-Vision Reading

**Citation:** Minakata, K., Eckmann-Hansen, C., Larsen, M., Bek, T., & Beier, S. (2023). The Effect of Serifs and Stroke Contrast on Low-Vision Reading. Acta Psychologica (Amst).

**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36563495/

## Summary

Testing 19 patients with autosomal dominant optic atrophy (a low-vision condition) across four fonts varying in serifs and stroke-width contrast, the study found that low stroke contrast combined with serifs produced significantly better (lower) word-identification thresholds (331 vs. 345, d' = 1.47 vs 1.15) than sans-serif alternatives — i.e., serifed fonts with uniform (low-contrast) stroke width were most legible for this low-vision population. This is directly relevant to Seshat's inclusion of Atkinson Hyperlegible (designed with low-contrast, uniform strokes for low-vision readers) and suggests that stroke contrast/uniformity, not just the presence or absence of serifs, is the more decisive legibility variable for low-vision users — a nuance worth noting since it complicates any blanket "sans-serif is always better for accessibility" claim.
