# Serifs and Font Legibility

**Citation:** Arditi, A., & Cho, J. (2005). Serifs and font legibility. *Vision Research*, 45(23), 2926–2933.

**Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4612630/

## Summary

Fetched successfully (full text, via PMC). Using custom fonts that varied only in serif size (0%, 5%, 10% of cap height), the study measured legibility (size thresholds) and reading speed (RSVP and continuous reading) in normally sighted and low-vision (age-related maculopathy) observers. It found only an "extremely small" legibility advantage for moderate (5%) serifs on size threshold, no systematic effect of serif size on reading speed in either RSVP or continuous-reading conditions, and that inter-letter spacing had a much larger effect on legibility than serif presence — the authors conclude serifs may even interfere slightly with legibility once the additional spacing they require is accounted for. For Seshat, this is directly relevant to the serif vs. sans-serif choice among its five typefaces (Source Serif 4 and Georgia are serif; Atkinson Hyperlegible, Verdana, Inter are sans): it suggests the serif/sans-serif distinction itself is not a strong legibility driver, and that letter-spacing and other typeface-specific design choices likely matter more than presence of serifs per se.
