# Eye Tracking Study: Font Size and Type in Online Reading

**Citation:** Beymer, D., Russell, D. M., & Orton, P. Z. (2008). An Eye Tracking Study of How Font Size and Type Influence Online Reading. In Proceedings of the 22nd British HCI Group Annual Conference on People and Computers: Culture, Creativity, Interaction — Volume 2. British Computer Society.

**Link:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/1531826.1531831

## Summary

In a between-subjects eye-tracking study of 82 subjects reading online stories at varying point sizes in serif and sans-serif fonts, smaller font sizes produced significantly longer fixation durations (slower per-fixation processing, though not significantly slower overall reading), and there was no significant difference in reading speed between serif and sans-serif fonts, with serif reading only slightly faster. Age and English-as-first-language also produced significant eye-tracking differences. This supports Seshat's body font size range (11.5-13pt) sitting well above the smallest sizes shown to slow fixations, and reinforces that typeface family (serif vs. sans-serif) is a secondary lever compared to size for reading speed — consistent with offering multiple typefaces as a preference feature rather than a performance-critical default.
