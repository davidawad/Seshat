# Serifs and the Evaluation of Scientific Abstracts

**Citation:** Kaspar, K., Wehlitz, T., von Knobelsdorff, S., Wulf, T., & von Saldern, M. A. O. (2015). The Effect of Serifs on the Evaluation of Scientific Abstracts. International Journal of Psychology, 50(5), 372-378.

**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25704872/

## Summary

Across two online experiments (188 and 187 university students), sans-serif fonts increased reading speed, but serif fonts produced more favorable evaluations of the same scientific text on every rated dimension — a result the authors describe as running counter to the "perceptual fluency hypothesis" (i.e., faster-to-read is not the same as better-liked or more credible). For Seshat, this is a caution against assuming that whichever typeface reads fastest is automatically the "best" choice: font selection trades off raw reading speed against perceived quality/trust, which is a reasonable justification for leaving typeface as a user-selectable preference (Source Serif 4, Georgia, Inter, Verdana, Atkinson Hyperlegible) rather than forcing a single "optimal" default.
