# Accelerating Adult Readers with Typeface: A Study of Individual Preferences and Effectiveness

**Citation:** Wallace, S., Treitman, R., Huang, J., Sawyer, B. D., & Bylinskii, Z. (2020). Accelerating Adult Readers with Typeface: A Study of Individual Preferences and Effectiveness. In *Extended Abstracts of the 2020 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI EA '20)*, 1–9.

**Link:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3334480.3382985

## Summary

Fetched successfully (full abstract text, via browser). The study found that participants' preferred typeface was seldom the typeface that produced their best reading performance — 8 in 10 participants believed their favorite font would be their best, but the data showed otherwise, confirming "one font did not fit all." Reading speed varied by an average of 117 words per minute between each participant's worst and best typeface (roughly 10 additional pages per hour), a difference the authors call "of real-world significance." For Seshat, this reinforces that letting users pick from several distinct typefaces (rather than defaulting everyone to one "objectively best" font) is likely to produce meaningful reading-speed gains, and that users' stated font preference should not be assumed to match their most effective font.
