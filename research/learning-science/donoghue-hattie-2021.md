# A Meta-Analysis of Ten Learning Techniques (Donoghue & Hattie, 2021)

**Citation:** Donoghue, G. M., & Hattie, J. A. C. (2021). A meta-analysis of ten learning techniques. *Frontiers in Education*, 6, Article 581216.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2021.581216

## Summary

This meta-analysis quantitatively synthesizes the ten learning techniques originally reviewed qualitatively by Dunlosky et al. (2013), drawing on 242 studies, 1,619 effects, and 169,179 participants. It finds distributed (spaced) practice and practice testing (retrieval practice) to be the most effective techniques of the ten, directly supporting Seshat's core design choice to pair FSRS-based spacing with active-recall card formats (short-answer/cloze) rather than passive review. The paper also notes effects were driven largely by studies measuring surface/factual recall (the kind of outcome flashcard review targets) and that feedback presence was an important moderator — relevant to Seshat's confidence-calibration and answer-feedback flow.

**Effect size:** Confirmed via source (Table 1): distributed practice d=0.85, practice testing d=0.74, elaborative interrogation d=0.56. Overall mean effect across all ten techniques was d=0.56.
