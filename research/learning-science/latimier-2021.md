# Spacing Retrieval Practice Episodes Improves Retention (Latimier et al., 2021)

**Citation:** Latimier, A., Peyre, H., & Ramus, F. (2021). A meta-analytic review of the benefit of spacing out retrieval practice episodes on retention. *Educational Psychology Review*, 33(3), 959–987. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8

## Summary

This meta-analysis (29 studies; 39 effect sizes in the core comparison) isolates spacing as a variable within retrieval practice itself, rather than comparing retrieval practice to passive restudy. It finds that spacing out repeated retrieval attempts produces substantially better long-term retention than massing those same retrieval attempts back-to-back (g = 0.74) — meaning retrieval practice alone is not sufficient; the temporal distribution of retrieval events matters independently. A second analysis (54 effect sizes) found no reliable advantage of expanding-interval schedules over uniform-interval schedules overall (g = 0.034), with expanding schedules only pulling ahead as the number of retrieval exposures per item increased. For Seshat, this is the direct empirical justification for spacing FSRS-scheduled reviews apart rather than allowing rapid re-tests or cram-style repeated retrieval in a single session, while also cautioning against assuming an expanding-interval curve is inherently superior to a well-calibrated uniform one — the benefit of expansion is conditional on review count, not automatic.

**Effect size:** g = 0.74 (spaced vs. massed retrieval practice, subset 1, meta-regression with robust variance estimation); g = 0.034, not significant (expanding vs. uniform spacing schedule, subset 2).
