# Optimizing Schedules of Retrieval Practice (Rawson & Dunlosky, 2011)

**Citation:** Rawson, K. A., & Dunlosky, J. (2011). Optimizing schedules of retrieval practice for durable and efficient learning: How much is enough? *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 140(3), 283-302.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956 (bibliographic record verified against Crossref: authors, title, journal, volume and pages).

## Summary

Rawson and Dunlosky studied how much retrieval practice is enough, and how it should be spread out. Their line of work on successive relearning (practising retrieval of an item to a correct-recall criterion, then returning to it in later sessions) is the reason Seshat's Learn mode keeps a term in rotation until it has been recalled correctly rather than moving on after one exposure, and brings a missed term back soon with the correct answer shown. Learn then hands the real spacing to FSRS: only typed answers and misses are written to the review log, so the next review is scheduled by FSRS and not by the session.

Only the bibliographic record was verified when this entry was written (the full text was not read), so no effect sizes are quoted here.
