# Long-Term Effects of Metacognitive Strategy Instruction on Student Academic Performance

**Citation:** de Boer, H., Donker, A. S., Kostons, D. D. N. M., & van der Werf, G. P. C. (2018). Long-Term Effects of Metacognitive Strategy Instruction on Student Academic Performance: A Meta-Analysis. *Educational Research Review*, 24, 98–115.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2018.03.002

## Summary

Note: the year supplied in the source list (2014) could not be verified; the verified publication (same authors, same title, matching effect sizes) is dated 2018. This meta-analysis of 48 metacognitive-strategy-instruction interventions found the effect on academic performance was not only maintained but grew slightly at long-term follow-up relative to the immediate posttest, with low-SES students benefiting most at follow-up. For Seshat, this supports building in explicit metacognitive scaffolding (e.g., prompting learners to plan their approach or reflect on why an answer was right/wrong, not just record confidence) rather than treating confidence-rating as an isolated bolt-on feature.

**Effect size:** Hedges' g = 0.50 immediately post-intervention, g = 0.63 at long-term follow-up
