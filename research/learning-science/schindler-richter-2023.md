# Text Generation Benefits Learning (Meta-Analytic Review)

**Citation:** Schindler, J., & Richter, T. (2023). Text Generation Benefits Learning: A Meta-Analytic Review. *Educational Psychology Review*, 35, 44.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09758-w

## Summary

Note: the citation originally supplied for this file (Kasper, Ufer, Kauther, & Köller, 2020) could not be verified — no such paper exists under that author list. The paper matching the described finding and effect size (text generation, g=0.41) is Schindler & Richter (2023), "Text Generation Benefits Learning: A Meta-Analytic Review," published in *Educational Psychology Review*. This multi-level meta-analysis found that having learners generate to-be-learned text content (rather than passively read it) improves learning outcomes, with the benefit robust across narrative and expository texts and not attributable to extra time-on-task. The effect was strongest when the cognitive processes engaged by the generation task complemented those already engaged by reading the source text — directly relevant to Seshat's cloze/short-answer card types, which require learners to actively produce rather than merely recognize an answer.

**Effect size:** Hedges' g = 0.41, 95% CI [0.31, 0.52]
