# Multimedia Design for Learning: An Overview of Reviews With Meta-Meta-Analysis

**Citation:** Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Harris, N. R., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2022). Multimedia Design for Learning: An Overview of Reviews With Meta-Meta-Analysis. *Review of Educational Research*, 92(3), 413–454.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329

## Summary

This is an overview-of-reviews (a "meta-meta-analysis") synthesizing 29 systematic reviews covering 1,189 studies and 78,177 participants on multimedia instructional design. It found robust, positive, meta-analytic evidence for eleven design principles — including signaling, spatial/temporal contiguity, coherence (removing seductive details), modality, and segmentation — and five principles that reduce cognitive load; good design mattered more for complex material and system-paced (e.g., lecture-style) delivery than for self-paced formats. For Seshat this cross-validates card-authoring guidance: keep related text/visual elements adjacent (contiguity), cut extraneous decoration (coherence), break complex material into smaller chunks (segmentation), and use signaling cues to direct attention to the tested concept.

**Effect size:** Not a single pooled effect — reports per-principle meta-analytic effects across 29 reviews; largest benefits found for captioning second-language video, temporal/spatial contiguity, and signaling
