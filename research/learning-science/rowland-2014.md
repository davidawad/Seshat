# The Effect of Testing Versus Restudy on Retention (Rowland, 2014)

**Citation:** Rowland, C. A. (2014). The effect of testing versus restudy on retention: A meta-analytic review of the testing effect. *Psychological Bulletin*, 140(6), 1432–1463.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559 (also indexed at PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25150680/)

## Summary

This meta-analysis pools 61 experiments comparing testing (retrieval practice) versus restudy on long-term retention and finds a robust, positive testing effect overall, with recall-based tests (cued/free recall) producing substantially larger benefits than recognition-based tests (multiple-choice, old/new judgments). This is the core empirical justification for Seshat's recall-first design: short-answer and cloze card types, which require effortful, generative retrieval, are predicted by this review to produce more durable learning than passive restudy or low-effort recognition formats like simple MCQ. The review also identifies feedback (presenting the correct answer after a response) and sufficient retrievability as key moderators that amplify the testing effect — relevant to how Seshat should surface answers and calibrate confidence ratings after each review.

**Effect size:** Overall mean effect size d ≈ 0.50 (testing vs. restudy, pooled across 61 studies), with format-specific estimates reported as g ≈ 0.72 (cued recall) and g ≈ 0.82 (free recall) versus g ≈ 0.36 (recognition/multiple-choice), and g ≈ 0.39 for testing without feedback. Confirmed via secondary citation (Schwieren, Barenberg, & Dutke, 2017) rather than direct extraction of paywalled full text.
