# Similarity Matters: Interleaving Effects Depend on Material Type (Brunmair & Richter, 2019)

**Citation:** Brunmair, M., & Richter, T. (2019). Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. *Psychological Bulletin*, 145(11), 1029–1052.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209

## Summary

This multilevel meta-analysis (59 studies, 238 effect sizes, 158 samples) finds that interleaving items during study — as opposed to blocking by category — produces a moderate overall benefit for inductive learning, but the effect is strongly moderated by material type and the perceptual/conceptual similarity structure between categories. Interleaving helped most for visual/perceptual discrimination tasks (e.g., distinguishing painting styles by artist), had a smaller but still positive effect for mathematical problem types, was ambiguous/nonsignificant for expository text, and actually reversed — favoring blocking — for word-list/vocabulary-pair learning. For Seshat, this is a direct caution against uniformly interleaving all decks/topics by default: interleaving should likely be opt-in or material-aware (favored for discrimination-heavy visual/conceptual content, neutral-to-harmful for rote paired-associate vocabulary), rather than treated as a universally superior scheduling strategy alongside FSRS.

**Effect size:** Overall interleaving effect g = 0.42 (moderate, positive). Moderator breakdown: visual/painting-style materials g = 0.67 (largest); mathematical problem types g = 0.34 (small, but significant); expository text and taste-based materials — nonsignificant/ambiguous; word-list/vocabulary-pair materials g = −0.39 (blocking outperformed interleaving).
