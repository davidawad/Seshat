# Inducing Self-Explanation: A Meta-Analysis

**Citation:** Bisra, K., Liu, Q., Nesbit, J. C., Salimi, F., & Winne, P. H. (2018). Inducing Self-Explanation: A
Meta-Analysis. *Educational Psychology Review*, 30(3), 703-725.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x

## Summary

A meta-analysis of 69 effect sizes (from 64 research reports) on learning outcomes for participants who received
self-explanation prompts while studying or solving problems, versus those who didn't. Self-explanation prompts
ask the learner to generate their own inferences about causal connections or conceptual relationships — "why is
this true?" — rather than just reading someone else's explanation. Overall weighted mean effect size (random
effects model): g = 0.55. Effectiveness varied across 20 coded moderators (task type, subject area, education
level, inducement type, treatment duration), but the intervention was robust across a wide range of instructional
conditions.

Directly relevant to Seshat's optional post-reveal "why is that correct?" prompt: the learning benefit comes from
the learner generating their own explanation, not from reading the card's author-written `explanation` field
(which is a separate, passive feature — see the generation-effect and text-generation citations for why active
production beats passive reading in general). Kept opt-in and off by default because it adds a step to every
review, which not every learner wants to trade for the retention gain.

**Effect size:** g = 0.55 (random effects model, 69 effect sizes)
