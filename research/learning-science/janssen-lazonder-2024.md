# Meta-analysis of Interventions for Monitoring Accuracy in Problem Solving

**Citation:** Janssen, N., & Lazonder, A. W. (2024). Meta-analysis of Interventions for Monitoring Accuracy in Problem Solving. *Educational Psychology Review*, 36(3), Article 96.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09936-4

## Summary

Note: the year supplied in the source list (2019) and the suggestion of a longer author list could not be verified — the verified matching publication (same finding, same effect size) is Janssen & Lazonder (2024), two authors only, in *Educational Psychology Review*. This meta-analysis of 35 studies found that interventions aimed at improving students' monitoring/confidence-calibration accuracy in problem-solving tasks produce a small but reliable overall improvement; effectiveness depended heavily on intervention type, with whole-task feedback, metacognitive-knowledge training, and external standards helping, while interventions that merely changed the *timing* of confidence judgments could actually backfire and reduce accuracy. This is directly relevant to Seshat's confidence-calibration feature: calibration prompts should be paired with substantive feedback (e.g., showing the correct answer and criteria) rather than just asking learners to rate confidence at a different point in the review flow.

**Effect size:** Overall g = 0.25, 95% CI [0.12, 0.37]
