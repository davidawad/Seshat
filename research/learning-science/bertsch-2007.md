# The Generation Effect: A Meta-Analytic Review (Bertsch et al., 2007)

**Citation:** Bertsch, S., Pesta, B. J., Wiscott, R., & McDaniel, M. A. (2007). The generation effect: A meta-analytic review. *Memory & Cognition*, 35(2), 201–210.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193441

## Summary

This meta-analysis synthesizes 445 effect sizes across 86 studies comparing memory for self-generated information (e.g., producing a missing word, synonym, or answer) against memory for the same material when it is merely read. Generating an answer reliably produces better retention than passively reading it, and the authors further identify moderators (e.g., the effect is larger for incidental learning and for mixed-list designs) that shape how strongly the benefit manifests. For Seshat, this is the core empirical justification for recall-oriented card types — short-answer and cloze deletion, which require the learner to actively produce the answer — over passive-recognition-only formats like MCQ, since generation itself (not just correct/incorrect feedback) is the mechanism driving the retention advantage.

**Effect size:** d ≈ 0.40 across 86 studies (445 effect sizes), per the original abstract: "The size of the generation effect across the 86 studies was .40 — a benefit of almost half a standard deviation of generation over reading."
