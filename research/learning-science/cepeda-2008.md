# Spacing Effects in Learning: A Temporal Ridgeline of Optimal Retention

**Citation:** Cepeda, N. J., Vul, E., Rohrer, D., Wixted, J. T., & Pashler, H. (2008). Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention. *Psychological Science*, 19(11), 1095-1102.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x

## Summary

Across more than 1,350 participants tested at retention delays ranging from one week to one year, the study found that the interstudy gap that maximizes final recall is not a fixed number of days but scales with how long retention needs to last: the optimal gap ran roughly 20-40% of the eventual retention delay for a 1-week test, but shrank to roughly 5-10% of the retention delay for a 1-year test. This directly undercuts any scheduler design that assumes a single "ideal" spacing ratio — the ridgeline surface (gap x retention interval x recall) shows the best gap-to-delay ratio itself shrinks as the desired retention horizon lengthens. For Seshat, this is a strong empirical anchor for FSRS-style scheduling over fixed-multiplier spacing: since FSRS fits per-card/per-user difficulty and stability parameters rather than applying one static interval-growth ratio, it is structurally positioned to reproduce this delay-dependent optimal-gap behavior instead of the one-size-fits-all ratios used by older SM-2-style algorithms.

**Effect size:** Optimal interstudy gap ≈ 20-40% of a 1-week test-retention delay, declining to ≈ 5-10% of a 1-year test-retention delay (measured as gap/retention-interval ratio at peak final-test performance across the full gap x retention-interval "ridgeline" surface).
