# A Meta-Analysis of Richard Mayer's Multimedia Learning Research

**Citation:** Cromley, J. G., & Chen, R. (2025). A Meta-Analysis of Richard Mayer's Multimedia Learning Research: Searching for Boundary Conditions of Design Principles Across Multiple Media Types. *Educational Research Review*, 49, 100730.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100730

## Summary

Note: the year supplied in the source list (2021) could not be verified — the verified, matching publication (same authors, same overall effect size and principle ranking) is dated 2025. This meta-analysis pooled 92 articles / 181 studies / 591 effect sizes from Mayer's multimedia-learning research program to identify boundary conditions for his design principles. The largest individual-principle effects were for removing seductive details, the modality principle, personalization, the multimedia principle itself, sentence-level coherence, and self-explanation, with smaller but still significant effects for scaffolding, cueing, and embodiment — directly informing which multimedia/text-design choices Seshat should prioritize on card templates (e.g., cutting decorative "seductive" content, keeping prompts coherent and free of redundant narration).

**Effect size:** Overall g = 0.37; largest individual principles: removing seductive detail g = 1.00, modality g = 0.82, personalization g = 0.70, multimedia principle g = 0.68, coherence g = 0.63, self-explanation g = 0.46
