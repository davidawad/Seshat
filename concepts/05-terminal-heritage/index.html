Seshat is a client-only spaced-repetition app built on FSRS. No account, no server, no streak to protect — the deck on disk is the deck in your hands. Three real screens follow: the study loop you'll run hundreds of times, the deck list, and the calibration report that tells you when "sure" doesn't mean correct.

What does TCP's three-way handshake accomplish, and name its three segments in order?

you answered

Sets up the connection — SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK, agrees on sequence numbers.

correct answer

Synchronizes initial sequence numbers and establishes a full-duplex connection: SYN → SYN-ACK → ACK.

why

Agreeing on sequence numbers before data flows stops stale duplicate segments from an earlier, closed connection from being misread as current traffic — the handshake is a synchronization step, not a permission check.

/stats/calibration

Confidence calibration

You rate your own confidence before every reveal. This report compares what you felt against what you got right — the gap is the point.

seshat — stats — calibration last 90 days

$ seshat stats --calibration --since=90d

confidence reviews correct rate distribution
Guessed 142 61%
Unsure 289 74%
Sure 1204 82%

read: when you mark a card "Sure" and get it wrong 18% of the time, that's not noise — it's overconfidence the FSRS scheduler can't see, because it only knows your grade, not how certain you felt giving it. Watch the "Sure" row: the closer it sits to 100%, the more your gut can be trusted without double-checking.
